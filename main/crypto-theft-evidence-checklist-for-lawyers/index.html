The working brief

What evidence does a lawyer need for a crypto theft case?

Collect a complete transaction record, proof linking the client to the originating account or wallet, the communications and access events surrounding the transfer, and a chronology of reports already made. Then define the question for the investigator: where the assets moved, which services may have been reached, what can be stated confidently, and what further records or legal steps may be needed.

The FBI asks cryptocurrency complainants to provide wallet addresses, transaction IDs or hashes, asset type, amount, date and time, and related communications. NIST guidance stresses the preservation and management of digital evidence. FBI guidance for cryptocurrency scam victims · NIST digital evidence preservation guidance

Crypto theft client intake checklist

01
01

Asset, network and token

Record the asset exactly, the blockchain used and the token contract when relevant. “USDT” alone is incomplete because the token can move across several networks.

02

Transaction identifiers

Collect each transaction hash, originating address, receiving address, amount, fee and timestamp. Include earlier funding transactions if they help establish control.

03

Account and ownership records

Preserve exchange statements, deposit and withdrawal records, wallet setup records, purchase receipts and material showing who controlled the relevant account.

04

Communications and representations

Keep complete email files, chat exports, usernames, telephone numbers, domains, advertisements and documents. Record what induced each transfer.

05

Access and compromise evidence

Note unfamiliar logins, password resets, device alerts, remote-access sessions, malicious approvals, SIM changes or API activity without altering the source records.

06

Reports and responses

List exchange tickets, police or regulator reports, stablecoin issuer correspondence and any response received. Keep exact dates, reference numbers and attachments.

Preserve the record before analysing it

02
1

Keep original files

Retain source emails, exported chats, statements, images and device records in their original format where possible. Work from copies and document any conversion.

2

Record provenance

Note who supplied each item, when it was obtained and how it was transferred or stored. A clear trail makes later review easier.

3

Separate fact from instruction

Mark what the records show, what the client recalls and what a third party claimed. Do not let a scammer's labels become the case theory by default.

4

Protect confidentiality

Use the firm's approved channels and access controls. Seed phrases, private keys and passwords should not be collected for a tracing assignment.

Admissibility is jurisdiction-specific

A blockchain record can be public without automatically satisfying every evidential requirement. Counsel should set the relevant rules for authenticity, expert evidence, disclosure, privilege and chain of custody. The forensic work should be scoped around that legal framework.

Brief the investigation around the decision you need to make

03

An effective brief explains the intended legal use, the available evidence and the deadline. It lets the forensic team focus on the findings that could change your next step.

LEGAL OBJECTIVE

Define the question and forum

Identify counsel, the relevant jurisdictions and whether you need evidence for a criminal complaint, civil fraud claim, potential freezing application or exchange engagement. State any filing deadline and what has already been reported.

RECOVERY FEASIBILITY

Test the practical route

Provide the approximate loss, incident date, known transactions and any existing trace. The assessment should consider identifiable services, observable balances, evidence gaps and whether further investigative spend is proportionate to the potential recovery.

SCOPE AND FEES

Agree the work before commissioning it

Define the networks, starting transactions, reporting requirements and update needs. Recoveris provides a written scope and fee proposal before a paid investigation begins. Flag urgency at the outset so timing can be discussed against the actual work required.

INSTRUCTIONS AND HANDOVER

Keep counsel in control of legal strategy

Agree who instructs the investigation, who receives the report and how sensitive material will be transferred. Counsel determines legal strategy and any privilege arrangements; Recoveris supplies forensic analysis and technical support for the agreed next steps.

What a crypto tracing report for solicitors should contain

04

A legal team needs to be able to follow the reasoning from the client’s transfer to each material conclusion. Recoveris prepares structured forensic reports with a chronological narrative, transaction maps, service attribution and verifiable blockchain data, tailored to the agreed instructions.

  • Instructions and materials: the question addressed, starting transactions, records reviewed, relevant dates and scope exclusions.
  • Reconstructed asset flow: the path across wallets, swaps, bridges and services, supported by transaction hashes and visual exhibits.
  • Methodology and attribution: the data sources, analytical method and basis for linking an address to a service, with confidence and limitations stated.
  • Findings for the next decision: observable balances at the time of analysis, potential intervention points and records needed from third parties.

Blockchain forensic evidence for potential freezing applications

Counsel should specify what the intended application needs to establish. We can supply technical findings about the transaction path, service touchpoints and asset position; counsel assesses the legal test and prepares the application. A report does not itself freeze assets or establish an automatic right to disclosure.

Frequently asked questions

FAQ
What should a lawyer ask a client after a crypto theft?
Ask for the asset and network, originating and receiving wallet addresses, transaction hashes, amounts, dates and times, account records, communications, domains, device or access events, and any reports already made. Keep the client's explanation separate from the underlying records and identify gaps that still need to be filled.
Are screenshots enough to evidence cryptocurrency theft?
Screenshots can preserve useful context, but they should not be the only record when original emails, chat exports, exchange statements, transaction data or device records are available. Preserve source files and metadata where possible, and follow the evidential and disclosure rules that apply in the relevant jurisdiction.
What should I send when requesting a crypto tracing report?
Start with the incident date, approximate loss, asset and network, transaction hashes, relevant wallet addresses and a short chronology. Explain the intended legal use, jurisdictions and deadline, and identify any existing reports. Agree an appropriate channel for sensitive records. Recoveris does not need seed phrases, private keys or account passwords.
Will a blockchain forensic report be admissible in court?
Admissibility depends on the jurisdiction, procedural rules and purpose of the evidence. A report should explain its sources, methodology, findings and limitations so the analysis can be scrutinised. Counsel should specify the relevant requirements before commissioning the work and assess whether further expert evidence is needed.
Can a blockchain investigation identify the exchange holding stolen crypto?
It may identify an address associated with a known exchange or other service, but attribution must be supported and expressed with an appropriate confidence level. The service controls its customer records, and disclosure, restraint or freezing decisions depend on its procedures and the relevant legal authority.
When should a lawyer involve a cryptocurrency investigator?
Consider early forensic input when the transaction path is unclear, assets cross networks or services, a time-sensitive intervention point may exist, or the case requires a reproducible flow of funds. The lawyer remains responsible for legal strategy and should define the jurisdiction, purpose and evidential standard for the work.
